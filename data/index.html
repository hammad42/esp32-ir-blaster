Hardware self-test

Sends a known signal and listens for it coming back. Point the IR LED at the receiver — 10–30 cm apart, facing each other — or bounce both off a nearby wall. Checks the receiver, the transmitter and the wiring in one go, with no remote and no appliance needed.

Send a specific code instead

Transmits whatever you enter and reports what the receiver got back. Leave blank for the default NEC test. State-based air-conditioner protocols cannot be sent from a hex value — learn those from the remote instead.

IR monitor

Enables the receiver and reports whatever it decodes without storing it. Useful for checking the sensor is wired correctly. Leave it off during normal use — it keeps the receive interrupt running.

Air conditioner — built in

Pick your brand and the device builds the codes itself, checksums and all. No remote, no capturing, and every temperature and mode is available — not just the buttons you happened to press.

Save as buttons

Once a brand works, save the settings you actually use. Saved entries behave like any learned command — they appear under Remotes, work with schedules and MQTT, and are included in backups.

IR defaults

    

      
      
      
      
      
      
    

    

      Leave mark-excess at 0 unless a specific appliance ignores a
      replayed signal. Receivers report marks slightly long and spaces slightly
      short; setting 50–100 µs here removes that bias at send time.

Backup

    

      The backup contains every learned command with its full timing data, as
      plain JSON. Import posts one command at a time, so a large archive
      restores without ever exhausting the device's memory.
